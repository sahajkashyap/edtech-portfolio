# Audit — Lessons 91–128

**Date:** 31 July 2026
**Scope:** `sheets/lesson-091.html` … `lesson-128.html` (38 sheets)
**Auditor:** independent agent; did not build these sheets; briefed to find
what was wrong rather than confirm it was fine. Report-only — it edited nothing.

These are the longest and most advanced stories in the sequence — 155 to 195
words — so they are the ones most likely to strain the page.

---

## 1. Blocking problems

**None found.** I looked hard — fresh browser measurement of all 190 pages,
line-wrap probes, word-by-word decodability re-runs, and 14 stories read end to
end. Nothing in this range is wrong, misleading, or unusable in a way that
blocks handing these to a parent.

## 2. Should fix

**A. The grown-up sheet describes a layout these packets do not have — all 38
sheets.** Page 1, "About 20 minutes, in this order":

- Step 1 says *"Say the practice words at the top of the story page."* In all
  38 sheets in this range the practice words are on their own page (page 2);
  the story page has no words at its top.
- Step 4 says *"They write one sentence on the lines under the story."* The
  lines are on page 4, under the drawing box — not under the story.
- Steps are numbered write (4) then draw (5), but the child's page puts drawing
  above writing.

Severity: the parent's only instructions send them looking for things that are
not where the sheet says. A parent recovers, but this is the one page written
for them, and it is wrong three ways in the same box. Nothing here teaches the
child anything false.

**B. Heart-word practice is disconnected from the stories.**

- Lessons 91–95 show cards for **floor / son**, 96–103 for **eye / about**,
  110–115 for **laugh** — and none of those words appears in that lesson's
  story (checked programmatically; e.g. "laugh" is absent from all six stories
  of 110–115). The card logic is "newest heart words from the last 6 lessons",
  regardless of the story.
- 19 lessons (104–109, 116–128) all show the **identical checkbox list: where,
  who, woman, would, you, your** — the alphabetical tail of
  `allowedHeartWords[-6:]`. In 12 of those 19 stories, *none* of the six
  appears; the rest contain only "you"/"your".

Severity: the page is titled "Words first" and ends "The story is on the next
page →" — it presents itself as prep for this story. A child doing several
packets checks the same six boxes every time and preps words the story never
delivers. This is the same ritual-not-practice pattern the project already
fixed for third questions. Not harmful, but it wastes the child's warm-up.

**C. Story lengths drift from the agreed 155–195 words.** Under: **96 (120), 97
(121), 98 (122)** — a third short of brief — plus 107 (140), 99 (146), 100
(148), 106 (149), 95 (154), 102 (154). Over: **114 (203), 113 (200), 128
(196)**. DECISIONS.md claims the range now averages 162 (true, 163) but
documents no exception for the three ~120-word stories. May be a deliberate
vocabulary-constrained call; nothing says so.

## 3. Cosmetic

1. **Every sheet has one stray `</div>` before `</body>`** (55 opens vs 56
   closes, all 38). Browsers ignore it; no visible effect.
2. **The story font can fall back to a double-storey "a".** `.passage` inherits
   `body`'s stack `"Century Gothic", Verdana` — the sheets' own comment fixes
   exactly this for the worksheet classes by inserting Futura/Comic Sans, but
   the story, the main thing a child reads, was left off that list. Fine on
   this Mac; wrong on a machine without Century Gothic.
3. **Lesson 114 seam:** the cat steals the steak, yet later *"After the meal,
   Tom did the dishes"* — the story never says what anyone ate. A sharp
   seven-year-old will ask. Lesson 108's *"They jumped and jumped… wetter and
   wetter"* couplet is visible lengthening padding, though it scans fine aloud.
4. **Plot repetition:** three "mystery sound turns out to be a hidden animal"
   stories inside 13 lessons — 113 (pup in a box), 118 (cat behind a stuck
   door), 125 (cat in the basement) — plus lost-dog 111 and lost-pup 128. A
   child working the sequence will notice they have read this story before.

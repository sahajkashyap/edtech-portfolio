# What the audit changed

**Date:** 31 July 2026

The three reports in this folder were written by independent agents that did
not build the sheets. This file records what happened to each finding, so the
reports are not just a record that someone looked — they are a record that
something came of it.

Every claim below was re-verified after the fix.

---

## Fixed — blocking

**A checker that had never tested anything.**
`wrap_check.py` reported *"0 sheets with a wrapped story line"* for the entire
project. It counted rectangles on the line element — but that element is a
block, and **a block returns one rectangle whether its text wraps or not**. A
line in Lesson 85 had been visibly wrapping the whole time.

Fixed to measure a Range over the text, which returns one rectangle per line
box. Then, because a checker that reports zero is exactly what the broken one
did, a sheet with a deliberately over-long line was run through it to confirm
it catches one. It does. *Verify the verifier, especially when it agrees with
you.*

**A guarantee that was printed and false, on three sheets.**
Lesson 7 used **"as"**, Lesson 8 used **"is"**, Lesson 46 used **"both"**. All
three are letter-decodable, so the auditor passed them — but "as" and "is" have
an s that says /z/, and the curriculum teaches them as heart words at Lessons
11 and 9 for exactly that reason. Lesson 46 teaches *voiced* th and used a word
whose th is unvoiced. Those sheets printed *"nothing here needs guessing."*

- `early_hearts.py` added and wired into the gate: a word the curriculum
  teaches as a heart word cannot appear before that lesson, however decodable
  its letters look.
- `build_word_bank.py` fixed at the source — the bank had been *offering* "as"
  to a writer at Lesson 7.
- All three stories rewritten. Lesson 46's warm-up strip lost "both" and kept
  its two genuine voiced-th words.

## Fixed — should fix

**The page written for the parent described a sheet from three redesigns ago.**
Every story packet said *"say the practice words at the top of the story page"*
(they had their own page) and *"they write one sentence on the lines under the
story"* (the lines are under the drawing), and numbered writing before drawing
when the page puts drawing first. Wrong three ways in the same box, on the one
page a parent reads. Corrected, and the order now matches the pages.

**The heart-word block was mislabelled.** It shows the newest heart words in
the sequence — reasonable as cumulative review — but the page presented them as
preparation for the story about to be read, and in most lessons at least one
never appears in that story. The heading now says what they are: *this week's
words, not always in the story.* The selection was left alone deliberately:
cumulative review is the right content; only the promise was wrong.

**Lesson 5 was the one letter sheet without the fold-off structure.** It now has
the read-3-times row and the grown-up instructions below a fold line, like
Lessons 2–4. Its blend words also moved from one-per-row to flowing, which
bought the room and let them grow from 46px to 72px.

**Three stories ran short.** Lessons 96, 97 and 98 sat at 120–122 words against
145–165 for their neighbours. Rewritten to 163, 164 and 165 — and not by
padding: each gained the set-up before the trouble and a try that fails before
the try that works, which is the beat short stories skip.

## Fixed — cosmetic

- **A stray closing tag** on every story sheet. Tag counts now balance.
- **The story was the one thing left off the font stack** that guarantees a
  single-storey lowercase `a` — the form children are taught to write. On a
  machine without the first font it would have fallen back to a double-storey
  `a`, contradicting the handwriting pages. Pinning it revealed the type-sizing
  model had been calibrated against a font that was not rendering, so the width
  ceiling now carries headroom for a wider fallback. Type dropped a point or
  two on 95 lessons: **a slightly smaller line beats a line that wraps on
  somebody else's printer.**

## Left alone, on purpose

- **Lesson 2's fold-block example uses "mat"**, whose `t` is not taught until
  Lesson 4. It is adult-facing text explaining the method, not something the
  child decodes, and "mat" is the example the whole method is explained with
  everywhere else in the pack. Changing it per-lesson would make the parent's
  instructions inconsistent between sheets.
- **Repeated plots in 111–128** — three "mystery sound turns out to be a hidden
  animal" stories, two lost-pet stories. Real, and fixing it means replotting
  from scratch rather than editing. Recorded for a future pass.
- **Lesson 114's seam** (the cat steals the steak, yet the family eats a meal)
  and **Lesson 74's** (a penny lost in grass, dug from mud). Small continuity
  snags a sharp seven-year-old might catch.
- **Blank lower halves** on a few pages where the no-wrap width ceiling binds
  before the height does. Explained rather than wrong.

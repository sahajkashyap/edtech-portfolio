A passage that is 98% correct is broken

Building a correctness system for early-literacy text — where nothing ships unless something that didn’t write it can prove it’s right.

The problem

Decodable stories are the hardest text in early literacy to write. A child on Lesson 41 needs a story built only from the sounds taught through Lesson 41. If one untaught letter pattern sneaks in — a single silent e, one sh — the child can’t decode it, so they guess.

Guessing is the precise habit decodable text exists to prevent. So 98% correct isn’t 98% good. It’s broken. And checking by eye doesn’t scale to 128 lessons.

The result

Dictionary words wrongly judged readable at Lesson 41
First version of the checker15,764  (18.1%)
After three rounds of adversarial review57  (0.07%)

Out of 87,119 words. A 277× reduction. The 57 survivors are nearly all genuinely readable — digs, dogs, kids, mats, taps, quit, quiz. 71 regression tests, each one a word that beat an earlier version.

Architecture

The writer never checks its own work. That’s the whole design.

assessment tool's ┌──────────────────────────┐ curriculum ─────────────►│ build_sound_list.py │ │ + CURRICULUM_CORRECTIONS │──► sound-list.json │ + UFLI_UNITS │ (the rulebook) └──────────────────────────┘ │ │ writer ──── draft ────► audit_passage.py ◄────────────────────┘ ▲ │ │ ▼ └──── violations ──── exit 1 = do not ship │ exit 0 ▼ story judge → check-pages.py → ship
  • The rulebook is generated, not typed — read from the assessment tool’s own curriculum, so names and order can’t drift.
  • The checker has no stake in the story. It didn’t write it.
  • Judgement calls are visible — every inference is a note in source; every correction cites source against published standard.
  • Silence is never approval. Empty input returns “NOT CHECKED”, exit 1.

What the loop caught

Three agents, none of which wrote the code they attacked.

Round 1 · the checker was 18% wrong

It passed picnic, basket, rabbit, little, vein, gem. Causes, each found by attack rather than review:

  • Consonant clusters checked only at word edges — so anything with a cluster in the middle sailed through
  • No syllable limit at all — potato passed at Lesson 12
  • ei and uy missing from the rulebook — vein passed at Lesson 34
  • y-as-a-vowel never gated — my and happy passed 43 lessons early
Round 2 · the overcorrection was worse

Fixing round 1 traded false negatives for false positives — three bad enough to make entire lessons impossible to write:

  • No word containing q passed at any lesson, ever. Lesson 32 is the qu lesson, and it rejected quit, quiz, queen.
  • milk, silk, self, elf, film blocked for 45 lessons — ordinary blends mistaken for silent-letter spellings
  • Lesson 85 couldn’t use ea — the grapheme Lesson 85 teaches
An unwritable lesson is a harder failure than a bad word slipping through: the generator produces nothing and nobody learns why.
Round 3 · checking the source data itself

An agent verified the curriculum against the published scope and sequence. The lesson order was correct — but 17 entries carried typos. Four changed what a child would see:

Lesson 94 was the largest: the source had a lesson teaching a concept the published sequence does not contain, while omitting two real sound-spellings. The agent also found the unit labels weren’t the publisher’s at all — 14 contiguous units had been recorded as 8 that ran out of numeric order.

Decisions worth defending

Gate at the later sound, or mark the lesson

Where one spelling has two sounds taught at different lessons (ow in snow vs cow), an early gate lets an untaught sound through and a late gate makes the teaching lesson unwritable. Resolution: gate at first teaching, and mark the lesson as needing an approved word list. 31 of 128 lessons carry that flag.

Record what cannot be solved

lens and gets have identical spelling shapes; only a dictionary knows one -s is a suffix. Rather than fake a rule, these sit in a documented limitations list with the reason.

Pedagogy constrains layout, not the reverse

A K–2 child is still learning to hold a pencil, so writing lines are 80px and drawing boxes get their own sheet. When a page overflows, the fix comes out of adult text — never the child’s working space. That rule forced a 3-sheet packet to 4, which was the correct outcome.

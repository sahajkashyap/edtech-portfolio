The letter m says /m/

Lesson 2
m /m/

This is Lesson 2. It assumes your child has met the letters from Lessons 1–1 (a). If not, start at Lesson 1 — each sheet takes only a few minutes.

What this lesson teaches

Today your child meets the letter m and its sound /m/ — a hum, like the start of mountain. Remember: it is the sound that matters when reading, not the letter’s name (“em”). And today something big happens: with a and m your child can read their first word — am.

The one thing to get right — say the sound clean

Say “mmm,” not “muh.” Hold the hum with your lips closed and stop — no “uh” on the end. A child who learns “muh” will later try to read mat as “muh-a-tuh,” and blending becomes much harder. Clean sounds now make reading easy later.

The mouth cue — say it together at a mirror

m — press your lips together and hum: mmm. The sound comes down your nose — touch the side of your nose and feel it buzz.

Watch each other’s mouths in a mirror. Seeing what the mouth does makes the sound stick far better than hearing it alone.

How to run the writing page

Big first, small last. Sky-write the letter huge in the air with a straight arm, then finger-trace the big letters on the page, then pick up a pencil. Always start at the dot, and say the sound — “mmm” — each time a letter is finished, so hand and sound get glued together.

Backwards letters are completely normal at this age. Point at the start dot and try again — don’t make it a big deal. It fixes itself with practice.

Two games — no pencil, no paper

1. Thumbs up. Say words one at a time; thumbs up if the word starts with /m/. Try: moon, mop, milk, mud — and some that do not: sun, dog, apple.

2. Robot talk. You say a word in two robot pieces, they snap it together: “I say c…at — you say cat!” Then d…og, s…un, m…op. No letters needed — this one works in the car.

Five minutes a day beats twenty on Saturday. Stop while it is still fun.

In the letter hunt, all the other letters are a — last lesson’s letter — so the hunt quietly reviews it. On the picture page, name the pictures together first: the moon, the sun, a mountain, an apple, a sock, a star.

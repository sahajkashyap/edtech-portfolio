The letter t says /t/

Lesson 4
t /t/

This is Lesson 4. It assumes your child has met the letters from Lessons 1–3 (a, m, s). If not, start at Lesson 1 — each sheet takes only a few minutes.

What this lesson teaches

Today your child meets the letter t and its sound /t/ — a quick, quiet tap, like the start of tent. The sound is what reading uses, never the name (“tee”). With a, m, s and t your child can now read five real words: am, at, mat, sat and Sam.

The one thing to get right — say the sound clean

Say “t,” not “tuh.” It is the quietest sound so far — just a tap of air, then stop. “Tuh” feels easier to say loudly, but a child who learns it will read at as “a-tuh,” and un-learning that is much harder than learning it right today.

The mouth cue — say it together at a mirror

t — tap your tongue just behind your top teeth: t. Quick and quiet, and no “uh” after it.

Watch each other’s mouths in a mirror. Seeing what the mouth does makes the sound stick far better than hearing it alone.

How to run the writing page

Big first, small last. Sky-write it huge in the air, then finger-trace the big letters, then pick up a pencil. Always start at the red dot, and say “t” each time a letter is finished, so hand and sound get glued together.

Backwards letters are completely normal at this age. Point at the start dot and try again — it fixes itself with practice.

How to run the words page

Two kinds of dot. The red dot on the writing page is where the pencil starts. The dots under a word are its sounds — touch each one, say its sound, then slide along the arrow and say the word. Fuller instructions are on the words page itself.

Two games — no pencil, no paper

1. Thumbs up. Say words one at a time; thumbs up if the word starts with /t/. Try: tent, top, ten, toes — and some that do not: sun, moon, apple.

2. Robot talk. Say a word in two robot pieces, they snap it together: “I say c…at — you say cat!” No letters needed — this one works in the car.

Five minutes a day beats twenty on Saturday. Stop while it is still fun.

In the letter hunt, the other letters are a, m and s — every letter learned so far, all in review. On the picture page, name the pictures together first: a tent, the sun, a tooth, the moon, the number ten, a star.

For the grown-up. The dots under each word are its sounds — one dot per sound. Your child touches each dot and says that sound, then slides a finger along the arrow and says the whole word. The arrow matters as much as the dots: the dots show where the sounds are, the arrow says do not stop between them. Said with gaps, “/m/ … /a/ … /t/” does not sound like mat; stretched together, “mmmaaat” does. The dots exist because a small finger moves faster than a beginner can sound a letter out — they give it somewhere to land. If your child gets stuck, you slide and stretch it first and let them copy you.

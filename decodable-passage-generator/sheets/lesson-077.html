The Star Card in the Barn

Lesson 77
ar /ɑr/
Before you start · is your child ready for this one?

This is Lesson 77. It assumes your child has already worked through Lessons 1–76. Each lesson only uses sounds taught in the ones before it. If your child has not had those yet, this story will be too hard — not because of anything they did, but because the sounds have not been introduced yet. Ask their teacher which sheet to start with.

About 8 minutes, in this order

1. Look at the picture together. Ask “What do you see? What do you think happens?” This puts the story in their head before their eyes do the hard work.

2. Cover the picture while they read. It is there for understanding, not for guessing words. Uncover it when they finish.

3. Warm up on the words, check off the heart words, then read the story.

4. If they get stuck, don’t say the word. Ask them to sound it out or use their sound spelling — the words their teacher uses. Tap under each letter as they go.

5. Read it three times, then draw what happened. The drawing is the comprehension check at this age — no writing needed, and the picture shows you what they understood.

The last sheet of questions is extra. Reading and drawing is the work — stopping there is finishing.

If they want the questions

Optional. They work just as well asked out loud over the drawing.

1. What slipped into Dan's hand when he tipped the jar up?
A card with a big gold star on it. Point back to the line if they guess.

2. Why did Dan run to the yard with the jar?
He could not see what was in it in the barn. Out in the sun he could. The answer sits in the two lines above.

3. Find the line that tells you the card was Dad's when he was a kid. Read it to me.
The line near the end where Dad grins. Let them find it rather than hearing it from you.

Why you can trust this page · checked, word by word

Every one of the 105 words in this story can be sounded out with what your child has already been taught. Nothing here needs guessing. If they get stuck, the answer is always “sound it out” or “use your sound spelling” — never “let me tell you.”

The only words not sounded out are the 57 heart words taught so far — and even those are mostly regular. Only the marked part of each is learned by heart. The newest are where who woman would you your.

The Star Card in the Barn

Dan and Dad park the car at the farm. Dad and Dan step into the dark barn. Dan digs in a box of rags and jars. Then Dan spots a jar up on the top shelf. The shelf is far up, but Dad is tall. Dad hands the jar to Dan. Dan can not see what is in it.

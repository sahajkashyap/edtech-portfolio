The Third Turn

Lesson 82

Before you start · is this the right sheet?

This is Lesson 82. It uses only sounds already taught in Lessons 1–81. If your child has not done those lessons yet, this story will feel too hard — that is the sheet, not the child. Ask their teacher where to start.

About 8 minutes, in this order

1. Say the practice words at the top of the story page, then the heart words. A heart word has one part that cannot be sounded out — the little heart marks the bit to just remember.

2. Read the story. If they get stuck, don’t say the word. Point under the letters one at a time and say: “Say each sound, then say them fast.” It sounds like this: “mmm – aaa – p … map!”

3. Read it three times — they color a circle each time.

4. They draw what happened. There is no picture to copy — the picture they make from the words shows you what they understood. No writing needed.

When it does not go smoothly

They read a word wrong. Don’t say “no.” Point at the word: “Try that one again — say each sound.” Fixing it themselves is the win.

They guess from the first letter. Say “Check with your finger — say every sound before you say the word.”

They sound it out twice and still can’t get it. Just tell them the word and carry on. A few told words do no harm.

They are stuck on nearly every line, or fed up. Stop — that is fine. Try an easier lesson, or the same one tomorrow. Five happy minutes beat twenty cross ones.

If they want the questions

The questions page is extra — reading and drawing is the whole job. These work just as well asked out loud.

1. Why did Meg and Kurt have to take turns?
They had just one sled. It is in the second line.

2. What did Kurt do so they could spot the rock?
He set his red cap on top of it. Meg had tugged at the rock first, but it was stuck.

3. Which turn was the best one to read about? Read me that bit.
Any of the three. Kurt's third turn, when he misses the rock, is a common pick.

The Third Turn

Meg and Kurt run up the cold hill. They have one sled, so they must take turns. Meg gets the first turn on the sled. Then Kurt gets his turn on it. He bumps a rock and lands in the dirt. Dirt is all on his shirt and in his curls. Kurt is fine, and he hops up fast. Meg tugs at the rock, but it is stuck. Then Kurt sets his red cap on the rock. Meg can spot the red cap from the hill. Kurt gets a third turn and misses the rock. He grins and hands the sled back to Meg.

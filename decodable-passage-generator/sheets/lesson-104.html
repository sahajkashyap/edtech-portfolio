Before you start · is this the right sheet?

This is Lesson 104. It uses only sounds already taught in Lessons 1–103. If your child has not done those lessons yet, this story will feel too hard — that is the sheet, not the child. Ask their teacher where to start.

About 10 minutes, in this order

1. Say the practice words at the top of the story page, then the heart words. A heart word has one part that cannot be sounded out — the little heart marks the bit to just remember.

2. Read the story. If they get stuck, don’t say the word. Point under the letters one at a time and say: “Say each sound, then say them fast.” It sounds like this: “mmm – aaa – p … map!”

3. Read it three times — they color a circle each time.

4. They draw what happened. There is no picture to copy — the picture they make from the words shows you what they understood. No writing needed.

When it does not go smoothly

They read a word wrong. Don’t say “no.” Point at the word: “Try that one again — say each sound.” Fixing it themselves is the win.

They guess from the first letter. Say “Check with your finger — say every sound before you say the word.”

They sound it out twice and still can’t get it. Just tell them the word and carry on. A few told words do no harm.

They are stuck on nearly every line, or fed up. Stop — that is fine. Try an easier lesson, or the same one tomorrow. Five happy minutes beat twenty cross ones.

If they want the questions

The questions page is extra — reading and drawing is the whole job. These work just as well asked out loud.

1. What went wrong with the first cake?
It came out flat, as flat as a plate. Have them find that line.

2. How did Kate work out what the problem was?
She reread the plan and tried again. When that cake was flat too, she saw the pan was too big.

3. When is it good to reread a thing? Tell me why.
Any answer is fine. Listen for a reason - you missed a bit, you forgot a step.

Kate at the Preschool

Kate helps at the preschool down the road. She reads to the kids and hands out cups. The kids there had a big day soon. Kate had a plan to bake them a cake. Dad let her mix it up in the pan. Dad set the pan in the hot stove to bake. The first cake came out flat and sad. It was as flat as a plate. Kate had to reread the plan two times. She tried it again in the same big pan.

Kate at the Preschool — page 2

That cake came out flat as well. The pan was too big! That was it. She had to remake the whole cake. This time Kate got a small round pan. Dad set it in the hot stove for her. The next cake got tall and soft. Kate had a grin. She got a red plate. She set the cake on it and ran to school. The kids at the preschool ate it all up.

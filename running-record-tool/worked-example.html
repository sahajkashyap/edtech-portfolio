The reading

The part the percentage hides

Ninety-three per cent and Instructional is a fair summary, and on its own it tells a teacher to keep going at roughly this level. It does not tell them what to teach.

The pattern does. Three of Maya’s four errors are the same error.

She dropped the –s three times, on the lesson named for it. She got the base word right every time. She is reading the front of the word and letting the end go.

The cue tally says the same thing from the other side: Meaning 0, Structure 1, Visual 4. Every miscue was driven by what the word looked like. None were caught by whether the sentence still made sense, which is how A cat nap in a pot went past her without a flicker.

Her retell was marked Some of it: “A cat got in the pot. It got muddy. Then they cleaned it.” She has the story. She is not using it while she reads.

What you do on Monday

Not a repeat of Lesson 20. She can read every base word in it. The teaching point is the ending she is dropping, and the habit of checking that a sentence still means something. That is a five-minute small-group focus, not a re-test.

Then read her again in two weeks and see whether the –s is sticking. That is the whole argument for this tool. The marking takes about as long as the reading, so you can afford to do it often enough for the second reading to tell you something.
